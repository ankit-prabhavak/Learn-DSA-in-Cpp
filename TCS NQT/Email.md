
# Email Writing

## Scenario 1: Project Delay Due to Technical Issue

**To:** Project Manager

**Subject:** Update Regarding Project Delay – [Project Name]

Dear [Manager's Name],

I am writing to update you on the current status of [Project Name]. Unfortunately, I encountered an unexpected technical issue with our server database, which caused system downtime and prevented me from completing the assignment by the original deadline.

Here is a quick summary of the situation:

* **Current Status:** The core modules are fully developed, and I am currently resolving the database connectivity error.
* **Expected Completion:** I expect to finalize all testing and submit the completed project by [Date/Time, e.g., tomorrow at 5 PM].

I apologize for this delay. I am fully committed to resolving this issue promptly and ensuring the final deliverable meets our quality standards. Please let me know if you need additional details.

Best regards,

[Your Name]

Software Engineer

## Scenario 7: Reporting a Hardware or System Issue

**Situation:**

Your company-provided laptop is crashing frequently, preventing you from working efficiently on your project.

**Write an email to the IT Support Manager that includes:**

* Explaining the technical problem you are facing.
* Describing the impact on your daily productivity.
* Detailing the steps you have already tried to fix it.
* Requesting an urgent system replacement or technical inspection.

**To:** IT Support Manager

**Subject:** Urgent Technical Issue – Laptop Crashing Frequently

Dear [Support Manager's Name],

I am writing to report a recurring hardware issue with my company-issued laptop (Asset ID: [Asset Number]). Over the past two days, the system has been crashing unexpectedly with a blue screen error multiple times a day.

* **Impact on Work:** This issue is preventing me from running critical build scripts and causing data loss, which directly affects my daily project deadlines.
* **Troubleshooting Done:** I have already cleared temporary files, restarted system services, and updated the operating system drivers, but the problem persists.

Given the urgency of my ongoing project deliverables, I request an immediate technical inspection or a temporary replacement device so I can resume work without further delay.

Thank you for your prompt assistance.

Best regards,

[Your Name]

Software Engineer

1. **User Role Permissions:** The document specifies admin access for standard users in section 2, which contradicts section 4.
2. **Data Export Limit:** It is unclear whether the database query limit for reports should be set at 1,000 or 5,000 records.

Proceeding without resolving these ambiguities may lead to rework and potentially impact our target sprint deadline. Could we schedule a quick 15-minute call today to discuss these points and align on the next steps?

Best regards,

[Your Name]

Software Engineer

### Memory Tips for Your Assessment:

* **Opening:** Always start with *Dear [Name],* and state your main purpose in sentence 1.
* **Body:** Use bullet points for lists (e.g., 3 learned items, 2 achievements, current status) to hit word count effortlessly while maintaining clean structure.
* **Closing:** Reiterate appreciation or commitment, then end with *Best regards,* and *[Your Name]*.

When writing your email under the **9-minute timer**, follow this 4-step structure to guarantee you pass the **100-word minimum threshold**:

| Email Section | Formula | Sentence Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Purpose** | `Dear [Title], I am writing to [inform / request / report]...` | 1–2 sentences |

### 1. The "100-Word Guarantee" Rule of Three

To hit the mandatory 100-word limit without wasting time counting words during the 9-minute timer, structure every email into exactly 3 body paragraphs:

* **Paragraph 1: Context & Core Purpose (~25–30 words)**
* *Formula:* State why you are writing immediately in sentence 1.
* *Template:* `I am writing to [inform/request/report] regarding [Topic]. Due to [Reason], I would like to [Action Needed].`


* **Paragraph 2: Detailed Bullets (~50–60 words)**
* *Formula:* Convert the prompt's bulleted conditions into a structured list.
* *Template:* Include 2–3 distinct sub-points with bold labels (e.g., `Current Status:`, `Action Plan:`, `Proposed Timeline:`). Bullets add structure and quickly raise your word count.


* **Paragraph 3: Reassurance & Call to Action (~20–25 words)**
* *Formula:* State the next step and give a professional sign-off line.
* *Template:* `I am committed to ensuring a smooth transition. Please let me know if you need any additional details.`

### 4. Grammar & Scoring Trap Warnings

TCS NQT automated evaluation engines check heavily for mechanics. Avoid these common mistakes:

* **Placeholder Removal Trap:** Never submit placeholders like `[Manager's Name]` or `[Date]`. Always replace them with actual text during the exam (e.g., *Dear Mr. Sharma*, *by tomorrow at 5 PM*).
* **Subject Line Clarity:** Always write a concise, professional Subject line containing the main topic. Never leave it blank.
* *Bad:* `Urgent` or `Help`
* *Good:* `Technical Issue Update – [Project Name]`


* **No Casual Shortcuts:** Avoid informal chat phrasing, contractions, or slang (e.g., write *do not* instead of *don't*, *regarding* instead of *about*, *as soon as possible* instead of *ASAP*).
* **Punctuation Baseline:** Ensure a space follows every comma and period, and capitalize the first letter of every new sentence. Automated systems flag these mechanical errors.
